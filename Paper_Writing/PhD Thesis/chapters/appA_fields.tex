This appendix collects, in one place, every vector field used anywhere in
this dissertation: the six canonical critical-point fields of
Chapter~\ref{ch:first_order}, the fixed and sinking vortex fields of
Chapter~\ref{ch:zeroth}, and the steady double gyre of
Chapter~\ref{ch:second_order}. Where two chapters use a field with the
same name but a different functional form, both are given here as
distinct fields, and the difference is flagged rather than resolved.

\section{Six Canonical Critical-Point Fields}
\label{sec:appA:canonical}

Six vector field environments centered at the origin were used to evaluate
the three-robot critical-point navigation algorithms of
Chapter~\ref{ch:first_order}. Let $\rho = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and
$\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$ (this polar radius is written $\rho$
rather than the source paper's bare $r$ to avoid collision with the
orbital radial magnitude $r$ used elsewhere in that chapter's control
laws). The base fields are the vortex (pure rotational flow), the sink
(radial inward flow) whose negation $\mathbf{v}_{\text{source}} =
-\mathbf{v}_{\text{sink}}$ is the source (radial outward flow), and the
saddle, an axis-aligned saddle rotated by $45^\circ$ so that its stable
and unstable directions lie along the diagonals $y=x$ and $y=-x$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{v}_{\text{vortex}} = \begin{bmatrix} -\rho\sin\theta \\ \rho\cos\theta \end{bmatrix}, \quad
\mathbf{v}_{\text{sink}} = \begin{bmatrix} -x \\ -y \end{bmatrix}, \quad
\mathbf{v}_{\text{saddle}} = \begin{bmatrix} -y \\ -x \end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:appA:base_fields}
\end{equation}
The sinking vortex (spiraling inward) and spewing vortex (spiraling
outward) are linear combinations of the base fields:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{v}_{\text{sink-vortex}} &= 0.4\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{vortex}} + 0.15\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{sink}}, \\
\mathbf{v}_{\text{spew-vortex}} &= -0.4\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{vortex}} - 0.15\,\mathbf{v}_{\text{sink}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:appA:combo_fields}
\end{equation}
Every field in this section is affine (in fact linear) in $(x,y)$, so its
Jacobian is constant and the three-robot estimator of
Chapter~\ref{ch:first_order} recovers the critical point exactly on any
non-collinear formation, at any size. Table~\ref{tab:appA:summary}
classifies each field's critical point by the eigenvalue structure of its
Jacobian.

\section{Fixed and Sinking Vortex (Zeroth-Order Chapter)}
\label{sec:appA:idetc}

Chapter~\ref{ch:zeroth}'s fields are built from solutions of the Laplace
equation $\nabla^2\phi = 0$ (uniform flow, sinks, sources, dipoles, and
vortices), superposed using the linearity of that equation. The two
fields used on hardware are those of the testbed paper \cite{39}.

The fixed vortex, centered at $(\text{center}_x,\text{center}_y)$, is
\begin{equation}
r = \sqrt{(x-\text{center}_x)^2+(y-\text{center}_y)^2}, \qquad
\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(y-\text{center}_y,\,x-\text{center}_x),
\label{eq:appA:idetc_rtheta}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
u = r\sin\theta, \qquad v = -r\cos\theta.
\label{eq:appA:idetc_vortex}
\end{equation}
Since $r\sin\theta = y-\text{center}_y$ and $r\cos\theta =
x-\text{center}_x$, Equation~\eqref{eq:appA:idetc_vortex} is the linear,
clockwise solid-body rotation $\mathbf{v}_{\text{fixed-vortex}} =
(y-\text{center}_y,\,-(x-\text{center}_x))$, the mirror image (opposite
sense of rotation) of the vortex of
Equation~\eqref{eq:appA:base_fields}, and likewise regular (finite
Jacobian) at the center. Vectors are then rescaled to the range
$[0.3,1]$ before being printed as the testbed's HSV floor map.

The sinking vortex adds a point sink of strength $c_s = 0.2$ to the fixed vortex:
\begin{equation}
u \mathrel{+}= -\frac{c_s\,(x-\text{center}_x)}{r^2+10^{-6}}, \qquad
v \mathrel{+}= -\frac{c_s\,(y-\text{center}_y)}{r^2+10^{-6}},
\label{eq:appA:idetc_sinking}
\end{equation}
with the $10^{-6}$ term added to avoid division by zero at the center.
This added term is $-c_s\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\rho}}/r$ (pointing toward
the center with magnitude $c_s/r$), so the sinking vortex is
singular at the center, apart from the $10^{-6}$ regularization, unlike
the fixed vortex and unlike
Equation~\eqref{eq:appA:combo_fields}, which is finite everywhere.

\section{Steady Double Gyre (Second-Order Chapter)}
\label{sec:appA:double_gyre}

The steady double gyre used throughout Chapter~\ref{ch:second_order} is
the steady member of the Shadden family \cite{49}, shifted from the
canonical domain $[0,2]\times[0,1]$ to $[-1,1]\times[-0.5,0.5]$ to center
it on the origin. The shift is $x_f = x+1$, $y_f = y+0.5$, where
$(x_f,y_f)$ are field-frame and $(x,y)$ world-frame coordinates, both
non-dimensional. The field is
\begin{equation}
u = -\pi A\sin(\pi x_f)\cos(\pi y_f), \qquad
v = \pi A\cos(\pi x_f)\sin(\pi y_f),
\label{eq:appA:dg_u}
\end{equation}
with $A = 0.1$. The separatrix is the line $x=0$, the saddle points are
$\mathbf{p}_1^* = (0,-0.5)$ and $\mathbf{p}_2^* = (0,0.5)$, and the gyre
centers are $(\pm0.5,0)$.

The Jacobian entries are $u_x = -\pi^2A\cos\pi x_f\cos\pi y_f = -v_y$ and
$u_y = \pi^2A\sin\pi x_f\sin\pi y_f = -v_x$ (subscripts denote partial
derivatives), so the flow is incompressible. Its determinant, gradient,
and Hessian are
\begin{equation}
D(\mathbf{p}) = -\tfrac12\pi^4A^2\bigl(\cos2\pi x_f + \cos2\pi y_f\bigr),
\label{eq:appA:det_analytic}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\nabla D = \pi^5A^2\begin{bmatrix}\sin2\pi x_f\\\sin2\pi y_f\end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf{H}_D = 2\pi^6A^2\begin{bmatrix}\cos2\pi x_f & 0\\0 & \cos2\pi y_f\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:appA:gradhess_analytic}
\end{equation}
The Hessian is diagonal everywhere, so its eigenvectors lie on the
coordinate axes and $D$ is additively separable in $x_f$ and $y_f$. $D$
is positive at the gyre centers, negative at the corner saddles, and zero
on the diamonds $x_f\pm y_f\in\tfrac12+\mathbb{Z}$. Along the separatrix
$D = -\pi^4A^2\sin^2\pi y$, deepest at the two saddles and rising to a
crest, $D=0$, at the origin.

The vorticity is $\omega = -2\pi^2A\sin\pi x_f\sin\pi y_f$, which vanishes
exactly on the trench grid of $D$ (where $x_f$ or $y_f$ is an integer).
There $\mathbf{J} = \mathbf{S}$ (the Jacobian equals its symmetric part)
and, by incompressibility, $D = -s_1^2$. The shear strain vanishes
identically on this flow, so $\mathbf{S} = \operatorname{diag}(u_x,-u_x)$
everywhere and the smaller strain eigenvalue has the closed form
\begin{equation}
s_1 = -\pi^2A\,\bigl|\cos\pi x_f\cos\pi y_f\bigr|,
\label{eq:appA:s1_analytic}
\end{equation}
with minima $s_1 = -\pi^2A$ at $\mathbf{p}_1^*$, $\mathbf{p}_2^*$, and the
four domain corners. By incompressibility the Okubo-Weiss parameter is
$Q = -D$ \cite{43,44}, so the $Q=0$ and $D=0$ contours coincide.
