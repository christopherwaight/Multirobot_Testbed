This appendix collects the statistical methodology actually used to
produce the trial counts, success rates, and error statistics reported in
Chapters~\ref{ch:zeroth}, \ref{ch:first_order}, and
\ref{ch:second_order}. Each section is specific to the estimator and
experiment design of its chapter; no single method (for example, a
confidence interval or a hypothesis test) is shared across all three.

\section{Zeroth-Order Chapter: Formation-Maintenance and Steady-State Statistics}
\label{sec:appF:zeroth}

The testbed paper \cite{39} reports trial statistics
for the fixed-vortex and sinking-vortex fields
(Appendix~\ref{app:fields}) on physical hardware. A 3-robot cluster with
fixed formation parameters ($p=0.35$~m, $q=0.35$~m, $\beta=1.05$~rad,
Appendix~\ref{app:kin3}) was used to test two control primitives
(vector-sum and vector-to-scalar) across the fixed-vortex and
sinking-vortex environments, with OptiTrack recording robot positions at
10~Hz. For each environment-primitive combination, 10 trial runs were
conducted; the fixed vortex was tested with both primitives, the sinking
vortex with the vector-sum primitive only. Both consistent and randomized
starting positions were used. Three metrics were evaluated: the robot
trajectory relative to the expected path, the steady-state error between
the final cluster position and the target position, and the cluster's
ability to maintain its commanded shape.

The cluster settled approximately 0.1~m from the center in the sinking
vortex under the vector-sum primitive, and within 0.2~m of the center in
the fixed vortex under the vector-to-scalar primitive; in both cases the desired location fell within the
circumscribed triangle of the three-robot cluster. Formation-maintenance
statistics (mean and standard deviation of each shape variable, across
the 10 trials per combination) are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:appF:formation}. Across all three test conditions, the
shape variables stayed within 1~cm of their commanded values.

\section{First-Order Chapter: Hardware and Simulation Trial Statistics}
\label{sec:appF:first_order}

Two error statistics are reported throughout Chapter~\ref{ch:first_order}
for convergence trials: systematic bias, the mean offset of the final
centroid position from the true critical point, and precision $\sigma$,
the standard deviation of the final centroid position, quantifying
repeatability. Both are computed from unrounded final positions.

\textbf{Simulation.} For each of eight fields (the six analytical fields
of Appendix~\ref{sec:appA:canonical} plus two fields reconstructed from
sensed hardware measurements), the cluster was initialized at a random
position drawn uniformly from a $1\text{ m}\times1\text{ m}$ box centered
on the true critical point, and the simulation was run for 100 time steps
at 10~Hz. This was repeated 1000 times per field. A trial was counted as
convergent if the trajectory remained bounded and terminated within the
stiction-limited neighborhood of the critical point; the final-position
statistics quantify that neighborhood. The cluster converged in 100\% of
the 1000 trials for every field tested.

\textbf{Hardware.} For each of two fields (vortex and saddle), eight
starting locations were selected around the perimeter of the workspace,
and the attraction controller was run 10 times from each location using
the three-robot equilateral-triangle formation, yielding 80 trials per
field (160 total). Due to file-management errors, 2 saddle trials and 1
vortex trial were lost, giving 157 usable convergence trials (78 saddle,
79 vortex). The cluster converged to the critical point in 100\% of these
157 hardware trials.

\textbf{Orbital control.} For each of six commanded radii (matched
between simulation and hardware), the orbital controller was run and the
mean radial error and standard deviation of the traveled path relative to
the desired path were recorded. In simulation, the controller ran for 600
time steps, discarding the first 100 (10~s) as a settling transient before
computing statistics. On hardware, the controller ran for 3000 cycles at
10~Hz (more than three complete orbits), and the reported statistics cover
the full record, including the initial transient (no settling-transient
discard on hardware).

\section{Second-Order Chapter: Monte Carlo Success-Rate Sweeps}
\label{sec:appF:second_order}

Chapter~\ref{ch:second_order}'s controllers are evaluated with Monte
Carlo sweeps over measurement noise $\sigma_{uv}$ and position noise
$\sigma_p$, run independently (one swept with the other held at zero),
from one straddling start, $(0,0.35)$ on the double-gyre benchmark
(Appendix~\ref{sec:appA:double_gyre}), at $10^4$ trials per noise level,
with independent random initial headings across trials. Noise-sweep
trials run up to 500 steps and stop at task completion or domain exit.

Success is defined as contact within 0.06 of the far saddle
$\mathbf{p}_1^* = (0,-0.5)$, a target that a noise-free run from this
start reaches under either primitive, without formation collapse (defined
as an RMS error above $4\rho$, with $\rho$ the formation radius, in the
fifteen inter-robot distances). Each trial also records straddle
retention: a trial straddles when the robots fall on both sides of the
separatrix at some point, and retention requires that the straddle, once
achieved, never be lost for the remainder of the trial. Both success and
straddle-retention rates are reported as simple fractions $\hat{P}$ of the
$N = 10^4$ independent trials at each noise level. Treating each trial as
a Bernoulli outcome, the standard error of a reported rate is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{SE}(\hat{P}) = \sqrt{\frac{\hat{P}(1-\hat{P})}{N}}
  \le \frac{1}{2\sqrt{N}} = 0.005,
\label{eq:appF:binomial_se}
\end{equation}
so every reported rate is resolved to within about one percentage point
at 95\% confidence ($\pm 1.96\,\operatorname{SE}$), and differences
between curves larger than this are not sampling artifacts.
%% NOTE(new-math): Draft_11 reports point fractions only; this bound is
%% computed from N alone, not from the data.

Clean (noise-free) trials, used to characterize acquisition and
traversal rather than success rate, run 400 to 600 steps and stop at
domain exit or, for the $D$ tracker, 150 steps after first saddle
contact; six matched starting positions were used, spanning both gyre
halves, the origin, and points up to $2.7\rho$ off the separatrix.

The Santa Barbara Channel trial (Chapter~\ref{ch:second_order}) is a
single 28-hour run per tracker from a shared start, not a Monte Carlo
sweep; no noise level is estimated for it, since the exactly determined
six-robot fit leaves no residual on gridded field data, so the noise
model above is validated on the double-gyre benchmark only.
