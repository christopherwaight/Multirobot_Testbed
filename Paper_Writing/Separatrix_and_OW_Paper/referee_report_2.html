Summary judgement

The over-telling is gone. The meander moved to the front.

I reviewed the previous version of this manuscript. The single largest problem then was that a four-page Discussion re-narrated three pages of Results, with 232 words repeated verbatim between them. That is fixed, and fixed properly. Sections VI and VII are now one section organized by experimental condition, and the strict-verbatim duplication inside it measures zero eight-word runs. The recommendation moves from major revision to minor.

What the cut exposed is a second problem that was always there and was previously hidden behind the first. Sections I through V are 8,280 words, 66.6% of the body, and the first number a reader can act on still arrives on page 11 of 18. Cutting the back of the paper did not move the front of it. A referee reading straight through now spends ten pages on setup for five pages of evidence, and the ratio is more conspicuous than before, not less, because the evidence section no longer pads the second half.

The heaviest single block is Section IV, Adaptive Navigation Controllers, at 2,496 words across pages 7 to 9, 20.1% of the body. The second is Section V-D, the Test Plan, at 682 words, more than half of Section V, which announces Section VI's experiments in Section VI's own terms. Neither is badly written. Both are simply larger than the argument needs.

On style: the register question has inverted since the last reading. The merged Results and Discussion is now the section that reads closest to the Introduction (mean sentence 23.2 against the Introduction's 23.0). The two sections that no longer sound like the rest are Section V and the Conclusion, both of which run long sentences with no short ones at all. Details and numbers in §08.

Five items carried over from the previous report are still open and still need authors rather than editors: the settle-rate evidence behind the sign-inversion finding, O(Ro), the τ collision, the abstract's two-costs claim, and the 18 uncited numbered equations. Two of those five have a cheap resolution I had not spotted last time, noted in §05.

01What works

Listed first because §11 is aggressive, and these have to come through it intact. The first item is new since the last reading.

New

The merged section is organized the way the argument is

VI

Condition-major rather than tracker-major was the right call and it does more than remove duplication. Estimator accuracy, clean runs, noise, rotating observer, measured field, selection rule. Every subsection now puts both trackers under one changed condition and reports what changed, which is exactly the comparison the paper exists to make. The selection rule that closes it was previously buried mid-Discussion on page 17 and is now a named subsection a reader can find in the table of contents.

One consequence worth noticing: the paper's structure now matches its own abstract. The abstract promises two surrogates, one estimator, and a priced tradeoff, and the reader meets them in that order with nothing between.

Keep

The splitting identity as the paper's spine

II-C, eq (12)

Deriving both surrogates as the two terms of one exact identity, then giving each a controller, is what makes this one paper instead of two. Equation (13) isolating the frame artifact entirely in the spin term is the payoff and it lands. The three consequences listed after (12) are the correct three and they organize the rest of the paper exactly as promised.

Keep

Minimality, the conic criterion, the pentagon corollary

III-A

Three results, six lines of proof between them, immediately useful to anyone building a formation. "Any six robots on one circle are singular, a regular hexagon for example, regardless of size or orientation. Moving one robot to the center removes the degeneracy entirely" is the kind of sentence referees remember and cite. See §05 for the one thing wrong with it, which is that the proposition is never referred to again.

Keep

Remark 1: an impossibility that dictates a design and then shows up as a number

III-C

No usable Hessian of s1 exists under a quadratic fit, for every field and every formation. That forces the gradient channel, which forces a fixed gain, which shows up as 0.0075 against 0.0016 and again as the discrete-stability crossing in Table IV. Theory to design to measurement in three moves, with nothing hand-waved between them. Still the best-argued thread in the paper, and the merge improved it: the measurement and the impossibility are now four paragraphs apart instead of nine pages.

Keep

The rotating-frame experiment, in closed loop

V-D-3, VI-D, Fig 7

Same physical flow, two observer frames, 0.025 against 1.219. Most objectivity papers stop at the algebra. Running it closed-loop, and justifying Ω = 0.2 against both the gyre-core vorticity (20%) and the command authority (Ωr ≤ 0.10 against kcmax = 0.12), answers the two obvious objections before they are raised. Reporting both trackers inside one subsection, which the merge made possible, is a clear improvement over filing the D tracker's 1.219 under a heading named for the s1 tracker.

Keep

Equation (37): naming the four terms the model cannot see

III-D

Writing Dxx out in true derivatives and then saying the four third-order terms "are set to zero by construction, not made small by a good fit" is unusually honest, and it is load-bearing twice: it explains the Hessian floor and it explains why the D tracker has no terminal test. Keep the sentence verbatim.

Keep

Table I, assumptions against the nearest prior work

I-A

Six rows, every cell sourced from the cited papers' own text, and the two rows that are premises rather than details called out as such. Disarming, and it does the positioning work that a head-to-head comparison cannot do here (correctly explained in VI-G).

Keep

The commitments paragraph

VI-C, closing

"Robustness is set less by the per-cycle accuracy of what a law reads than by how many of its commitments are unrevisable" is still the most valuable sentence in the paper, and it survived the trim with both supporting moves intact: the 0.6% against 1.7% recomputation against the right denominator, and the observation that the near-total flip follows from the noise being comparable to the signal the decision reads. Keeping this as the one epigram, and converting the other three, was the right selection.

02The narrative arc

Six items. A1 through A3 are new to this reading. A4 through A6 are carried over and still open.

A1

Two thirds of the paper is setup, and the merge did not change that

global

Sections I through V total 8,280 words against Sections VI and VII's 4,152. That is 66.6% of the body before the first result. In page terms the first result is on page 11 of 18, which is where it was on page 11 of 19 last time. The Discussion cut removed a page from the back of the paper and left the front untouched, so the ratio got worse, not better.

This is not an argument that the technical sections are wrong. They are the strongest writing in the manuscript and I said so last time. It is an argument that a referee opening this paper reads for ten pages without being shown that any of it works, and that is the meander now.

FixLedger items 1, 2 and 3 in §11, which are all in Sections IV, V and the Appendix. Roughly 1,050 words out of the front matter, which brings the setup share to about 60% and the first result to page 9 or 10.
A2

Section V-D announces Section VI in Section VI's words

V-D, 682 w

The Test Plan is 682 words, more than half of Section V's 1,268. It states the six clean starts, the noise grid, the success criterion, the straddle-retention metric, the exit box, the rotating-frame protocol, the ocean protocol, and the acquisition sweep. Section VI then consumes all eight, and in four cases restates the protocol before reporting the number.

The specific overlaps I can still measure: the acquisition sweep (grid, σp levels, trials per cell, start offset) is stated in V-D-5 and again in VI-C; the exit box is stated in V-D-2 and again in VI-C's footnote; the 0.0005 resolution spacing is in V-D-2, Fig. 8's caption, and VI-C.

FixFold V-D-1, V-D-2 and V-D-5 into the paragraphs of VI that consume them. Keep V-D-3 and V-D-4, which state protocol a reader genuinely needs before the result, and which are short. This is the same recommendation I made last time and it is now the largest single item left.
A3

The same experiment is billed two different ways

V-D-5 vs VI-A

V-D-5 files the estimator-accuracy sweep under "Supporting checks", one of three checks that "sit alongside the four families". Section VI then opens with it, as subsection A, ahead of every closed-loop result. A reader who took V-D at its word arrives at VI expecting clean runs and gets 302 words of open-loop validation instead.

There is a second oddity underneath. VI-A contains no discussion at all. It is pure validation of Section III-D's predictions, and it sits five pages downstream of the predictions it validates. In a section titled Results and Discussion, the lead subsection discusses nothing.

Two options, and I prefer the first. (1) Move VI-A's first two paragraphs into III-D, next to the error budget and the gain ladder they confirm, and let Section VI open on the clean runs. That removes a subsection, kills the billing mismatch, and puts each prediction next to its check. Cost: Section III grows by about 200 words while Section VI loses 300. (2) Leave it, and rewrite V-D-5 so the estimator sweep is billed as the first result rather than as a supporting check. Cheaper, but it leaves a discussion-free subsection at the head of a discussion section.
A4

The sign-inversion finding still rests on a figure caption

VI-C, Fig 8 · carried over

VI-C: "Runs on either side of the transition settle at a saddle with comparable reliability and differ in which one." Figure 8's two panels plot far-saddle success and straddle retention. Neither shows a settle rate. The claim appears in the caption's closing clause and in the sentence next to it, and nowhere else in the paper.

New this reading. The evidence exists, or existed. The review of Draft 5c cites "Table II(b) against Table II(c)" and quotes the number directly: far-saddle success 0.0% at σuv = 0.005 while 90.9% of those same runs settle cleanly at a saddle. That column was in the manuscript two drafts ago and came out during the trimming. It is the single most informative number in the noise sweep, because it is what converts a 0.0% into a diagnosis.

FixRestore the settle rate as a third column of Table II, or give it inline in VI-C with the same provenance the rest of the sweep has. Do not leave a caption as the sole source for the paper's most quotable finding. Confirm the 90.9% against the current trial records before it goes back in; it is quoted here from a review of an earlier draft, not measured from this one.
A5

Limitations and Future Work sit inside Results and Discussion

VI-G, VI-H

Together 681 words, 5.5% of the body, as subsections G and H of a section titled Results and Discussion. None of the seven T-RO and Transactions comparators in §10 does this; where they carry limitations at all it is inside the conclusion or inside the results paragraph that earns it.

I raise it and then leave it. The argument for the current arrangement is real: the limitations here are mostly conditions on results that the same section just reported, and splitting them off would mean naming those results twice. If it stays, VI-G's first paragraph should stop opening on the general claim about trenches versus level sets, which is a Section II observation arriving fifteen pages late.

A6

The abstract runs 310 words and rounds a number the body resolves

abstract

310 words against an IEEE norm of 150 to 250. It still carries the units housekeeping sentence ("Double-gyre quantities are reported in the field's non-dimensional world units throughout") which belongs in Section II-D, where the identical statement already appears.

It also says the D tracker's cliff is at σuv ≈ 0.01 and the s1 tracker's at ≈ 0.002. The body resolves the first to 0.0079, and makes a point of it, because 0.0079 against the estimator's 0.0075 is a 5% agreement and the whole "the estimator sets the ceiling" argument turns on it. Rounding to 0.01 in the abstract throws that away and reads as a different number.

FixDrop the units sentence, drop one of the two failure-cliff numbers, and quote the surviving one as 0.0079. About 90 words, and the abstract lands near 220.

V3

What the register actually is, for whoever does the pass

reference

Sections I to IV share a specific pattern worth naming, since the fix in V1 and V2 is to reproduce it. A long sentence that carries the technical content, followed by a short one that states what it means. "The four third-order terms, the size of Dxx itself, have no counterpart among the twelve fitted coefficients. They are set to zero by construction, not made small by a good fit." Or: "The D tracker's rotating-frame run does not degrade. It leaves the structure."

The long-short pair is the voice. Section V has the long halves and none of the short ones; the Conclusion has the same problem across a whole page. No new material is needed to fix either, only breaks.

Two structural readings from the comparator set, beyond the Discussion point. First, the appendices that exist in this literature hold proofs, not standard material. Matveev carries four proof appendices; Briñón-Arranz carries one. This paper's Appendix A holds twelve equations of cluster-space kinematics fully cited to [37],[38] and derives nothing the controllers use, which is the opposite arrangement. Second, none of the seven spends 66% of its body before its first result. Yao reaches experiments at section VI of VIII but the preceding sections carry the convergence theorems that are the contribution; here the theory-to-evidence ratio is similar while the theory is spread over five sections rather than concentrated.

The diagnosis in one line, updated: the Discussion is no longer the problem, and the front matter now is. The previous reading measured a Discussion 67% longer than its Results. That ratio is gone. What replaces it is a setup-to-evidence ratio of 2:1 by word count and 10:5 by page count.
