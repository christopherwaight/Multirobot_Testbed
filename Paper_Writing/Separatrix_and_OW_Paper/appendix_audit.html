The arithmetic, and one honest caveat

Seven of ten items go. That is 70% of the appendix by item count, but only about 47% by length.

The two numbers diverge because the items that should go are, with one exception, the short ones: two-sentence proofs of things Section III already proves in the body, and a three-sentence sketch that gestures at a computation instead of performing it. Together they are only about a fifth of the appendix's length. The bulk of the appendix is the three proofs the paper's claims actually rest on, and those cannot go: Theorem 1 is the only global Lyapunov certificate in the manuscript, Theorem 2 is the D tracker's central claim, and Proposition 2 is the s1 tracker's central claim and the theoretical half of the paper's headline result.

So a 75% length cut is not reachable without deleting a proof a referee will want. What is reachable, and what this document delivers, is 47% — and the survivors come out clearer, not just shorter, because roughly a third of their current length is redundancy with the body, stale references to a controller architecture the manuscript no longer has, and one branch of Proposition 2 argued twice by two different routes.

If you need to go further, the one remaining lever is moving Proposition 2's proof to supplementary material, which takes the appendix to about 0.73 col (59% removed). I would not. The demonstration in Fig 6 is the evidence; the proof is the guarantee, and objectivity is the claim the paper is named for.

02Why each cut goes

Grouped by failure type, because the seven deletions are really three problems.

Problem one: three proofs are already in the body

A3

Proposition 1's proof is printed twice

III-A and App A

Section III-A states Proposition 1 and gives its proof immediately: Φ is singular exactly when Φc = 0 for some nonzero c, row i is φ(p′i)⊤, so this holds iff all six points lie on the zero set of the quadratic φ(·)⊤c. The appendix then gives the same argument again in three sentences.

There is no version of the paper in which both should appear. The body's placement is the right one, because a reader meets the conic criterion while thinking about formation geometry, not four pages later. Delete the appendix copy.

A4

The pentagon corollary is duplicated, and the appendix copy is the sloppier one

III-A and App A

The appendix argues: "The five vertices are in general position, so they determine a unique conic, their circumscribed circle." Five concyclic points are precisely not in general position for conics in the sense that phrase usually carries; the reason they determine a unique conic here is that any conic vanishing on five points of a circle must be a scalar multiple of that circle.

Which is exactly what III-A already says, correctly: "a conic vanishing at five points of a circle must be a multiple of that circle, and a circle does not pass through its own center." Keep the body's wording, delete the appendix's.

A2

Minimality is proved in the body too

III-A and App A

III-A: each robot contributes one equation per component, each system has six unknowns, so fewer than six robots leave the model unidentifiable and the solution set is a positive-dimensional affine subspace. The appendix restates this as a null-space argument. Same content, and the body version is the one a reader will actually encounter at the moment they care.

If you keep a numbered environment for minimality — and you should, since the abstract leans on it — number it in III-A on the same integer scheme as Proposition 1, and let the two-sentence argument sit inline beneath it.

Problem two: one item claims proof status without proving anything

A5

Lemma 1's proof is a description of a calculation, not the calculation

App A

The operative sentence is: "The remaining rows follow from direct inversion of Φ using the fifth roots of unity; the row norms reduce to the stated constants, and repeating the computation with an arbitrary ring phase leaves them unchanged." A reader cannot check any of that. It tells them a computation was done and what it produced.

This is the item most likely to read as filling space, because it occupies proof real estate while doing no proof work. The final sentence also restates the isotropic gradient covariance that III-D has already given.

The closed-form gains are a genuine contribution and belong in the abstract where they are. The right handling is to state them as a lemma in III-D, cite the open-loop verification already reported there (every coefficient's standard deviation matching within 0.3%, over 2×105 draws), and put the roots-of-unity algebra in supplementary material for anyone who wants to verify it symbolically. An empirical verification of an analytic claim at that sample size is acceptable in this venue; a gestured proof is weaker than either.

Problem three: two lemmas are load-bearing for a controller you no longer have

A7

Lemma 2 proves something the rewritten control law makes structural

App A

Lemma 2 exists to establish that "every selector mode applies the identical transverse contraction, so mode switching cannot chatter it." That was a real obligation when (26) was a three-case switch, because a reader had no way to see it without checking each case.

In the current IV-B there is nothing to check. The transverse term of (26) does not contain τ, so it is literally the same expression on and off the band, and IV-D already states this in the sentence beginning "The w2 term does not contain τ." The lemma now proves a fact that is visible by inspection, and its proof does so by enumerating "the attract, slide, and flow steps" — three objects the manuscript no longer defines.

What genuinely survives is three lines, and they belong in IV-D, not the appendix: the reduction to ṅ = −cmax tanh(n/cmax) under Assumption 1 (already there), the exponential rate estimate, and the footnote about the exact double-gyre form. See §06.

A8

Lemma 3's content is needed; Lemma 3 is not

App A

Unlike Lemma 2, this one carries something a reader cannot see from the law: the quantitative lower bound m on tangential speed, which is what makes Theorem 2(ii)'s finite-time claim LΓ/m mean anything.

But it is cited from exactly one place, the proof of Theorem 2, four lines further down. A lemma with a single consumer immediately below it is a paragraph wearing a costume. And its proof table has four rows — FLOW, SLIDE off-band, ATTRACT off-band, attract fallback — where the current τ in (25) has two branches and (26) has one degenerate case.

Fold it into Theorem 2's proof as Step 2, rewritten against the two branches that now exist. The bound (39) survives as a displayed equation there, and the derivation gets shorter, because both branch bounds are one application of the monotonicity of tanh.

03Rewritten: Proof of Theorem 1

The current proof is correct and compresses six distinct steps into four sentences, two of which contain three equalities each. Nothing here is new mathematics; it is the same argument with the intermediate steps restored so a reader can follow it without reconstructing them.

What it says now — the compression to fix Since ∇VA = HDg, V̇A = g⊤HDδ = −Σk vmax tanh(zk/vmax) g⊤HDwk = −Σk vmax tanh(zk/vmax) λk(wk⊤g), and λk(wk⊤g) = λk²zk, giving (31)… Three of the five gaps a reader has to fill are in that one sentence. Note also vmax, where Section IV writes cmax.
Replacement text — Proof of Theorem 1~185 words, from ~90

Proof of Theorem 1: Write δ for the signed Newton step, the degenerate case of (26), whose k-th eigendirection component is sat(−zk) = −cmax tanh(zk/cmax).

Gradient of the Lyapunov function.

Since VA = ½g⊤g with g = ∇D(pc), the chain rule gives ∇VA = (∂g/∂p)⊤g = HDg, using that the Jacobian of the gradient is the Hessian and that HD is symmetric.

Derivative along trajectories.

Differentiating along (30),

V̇A = ∇VA⊤ ṗc = g⊤HDδ.

Expanding δ in the eigenbasis and using HDwk = λkwk,

g⊤HDδ = Σk sat(−zk) λk(wk⊤g).

By the definition zk = (wk⊤g)/λk we have wk⊤g = λkzk, so each summand carries a factor λk²zk, which is (31).

Sign and equality case.

Every summand has the form λk² zk tanh(zk/cmax) with λk² ≥ 0 and z tanh z ≥ 0, vanishing only at z = 0. Hence V̇A ≤ 0, with equality exactly when z1 = z2 = 0. Because HD is nonsingular the λk are nonzero, so this forces w1⊤g = w2⊤g = 0, and since {w1, w2} is a basis of the plane, g = 0.

Convergence.

On a bounded trajectory along which HD stays nonsingular, VA is continuous, nonincreasing, and bounded below, so LaSalle's invariance principle gives convergence to the largest invariant subset of {V̇A = 0}, which by the previous step is the critical set {g = 0} of D.

Local rate.

Near an isolated critical point p⋆D with HD nonsingular, ‖g‖ is small, so |zk| ≪ cmax and the saturation is inactive to first order: tanh(x) = x + O(x³) gives δ ≈ −Σk zkwk = −HD−1g. Linearizing g about p⋆D gives g ≈ HD(pc − p⋆D), so δ ≈ −(pc − p⋆D): a linear contraction at unit rate in the rescaled time of (30), that is at rate k in real time. Local exponential stability follows.

What the expansion buys. Five things a referee currently has to supply: that ∂g/∂p is HD and symmetric; that wk⊤g = λkzk is a definition and not a derivation; that the equality case needs nonsingularity and the basis property; that "the invariance principle" is LaSalle and which hypotheses it needs; and that "unit-rate" is in rescaled time, not real time. The last of these is the one most likely to be queried, since Section IV-D now explicitly flags that the simulator runs at k = 3.0 and k = 1.8.

04Rewritten: Proof of Theorem 2

This is the biggest structural change. The proof absorbs Lemma 3, drops its four-row table, and stops opening with a pointer. Steps 1 and 2 are the two properties IV-D already identifies in the law itself, now carried through to the bounds Theorem 2 needs.

What it says now — the pointer problem "(i) is Lemma 2. (ii): by Lemma 3, ℓ(t) is monotone in the flow direction with rate at least m outside the r0 balls, and mode switches cannot reverse it…" — the first sentence of the proof of the paper's main traversal theorem is a cross-reference, and the second delegates to a lemma whose case analysis no longer matches the controller.
Replacement text — Proof of Theorem 2~330 words, replacing ~290 across two environments

Proof of Theorem 2: Work in the coordinates (ℓ, n) of Assumption 1 on the tube Tw.

Step 1: transverse convergence, giving (i).

The transverse term of (26) does not contain τ, so it is the same command whether or not pc ∈ B. Under the normal form (32) the Hessian is diagonal in (ê∥, ê⊥) with transverse eigenvalue λ⊥ > 0 and transverse gradient component λ⊥n, so the Newton argument in that direction is −λ⊥n/λ⊥ = −n and the saturated command is (33). The right-hand side is strictly opposite in sign to n, so V⊥ = ½n² is strictly decreasing off n = 0 and Tw is forward invariant. For |n| ≤ cmax, concavity of tanh on the positive axis gives tanh(n/cmax) ≥ n tanh(1)/cmax, hence V̇⊥ ≤ −2 tanh(1) V⊥ and n → 0 exponentially.

Step 2: tangential progress.

Both branches of (25) return τ ≥ 0, and w1 is signed so that v̂0⊤w1 ≥ 0, so the tangential command never opposes the ambient flow. For a speed bound, take the branches in turn. On the band, τ = v̂0⊤w1, and since Γ is flow-invariant with ‖v‖ ≥ fmin outside the r0 balls, τ ≥ fmin there. Off the band, τ = |w1⊤ĝ0|/|λ1| = |h′(ℓ)|/|λ1| ≥ mh/λ̄∥, using |h′| ≥ mh wherever the band test fails on Γ and |λ1| ≤ λ̄∥ on the tube. Since tanh is increasing, the realized tangential speed is at least

m = min{ cmax tanh(fmin/cmax), cmax tanh(mh/(λ̄∥cmax)) }.

Step 3: finite-time arrival, giving (ii).

By Step 2, ℓ(t) is monotone in the flow direction at rate at least m outside the r0 balls, and because Step 1 holds in both branches the band test cannot reverse it. Travelling a trench of length LΓ therefore takes at most LΓ/m.

Step 4: capture, giving (iii).

At the flow saddle D attains a local minimum, so the true HD ≻ 0 and its eigenvalues share a sign. The degenerate case of (26) is then active and the command is exactly the signed Newton step certified by Theorem 1, which gives local exponential convergence to that well; the well coincides with the flow saddle p∗.

Step 5: continuation when the test does not fire.

Step 4 concerns the true Hessian. If the sign test fails on ĤD,0 despite HD ≻ 0 holding for the field, (26) stays in its band or off-band branch. The saddle is a right-angle crossing of two trench segments (Section II-E), so whichever segment the flow direction and the sign convention on w1 select satisfies Assumption 1 in its own right, and Steps 1 to 3 apply unchanged with Γ relabeled. Remark 2 gives the conditions under which this occurs, and Section VI reports it at every saddle reached.

Note on Step 5. The current text says the tracker "falls through to Lemma 3's flow or descent case," which will not survive Lemma 3's removal. More importantly, this paragraph is the formal counterpart of a behavior the results section reports at every saddle, so it is doing more work than its position at the end of a proof suggests. It reads better as a labeled step than as a trailing clause.

05Rewritten: Proof of Proposition 2

The only survivor that gets shorter. It is currently one 400-word paragraph that argues the descent branches twice by two different routes, names five modes IV-C does not define, and closes with a sentence of discussion. The logical skeleton underneath is five steps and it is clean.

The redundancy to remove The current proof says "PARK, CAPTURE, and the transverse channel (28) apply the invariant gradient along n̂′ = Qn̂… all three output the rotated command Qδ," and then two sentences later says "ACQUIRE, PARK, and CAPTURE are the vector-saturated descent −sat(g⊥∇ŝ1) of (28), which preserves the direction of the co-rotating invariant gradient and is therefore exactly covariant." PARK and CAPTURE are covered twice, by two different arguments. The second is the correct and general one; the first is a remnant of the era when the saturation acted componentwise and each branch needed separate treatment. With vector saturation in (28), one argument covers every branch at once.
Replacement text — Proof of Proposition 2~300 words, from ~400

Proof of Proposition 2: Let the observer rotate as pQ = Q(t)p.

Step 1: what transforms how.

The transformed velocity gradient is JQ = QJQ⊤ + Q̇Q⊤. The transport term Q̇Q⊤ is antisymmetric, so it lands entirely in the spin part and the strain part transforms by similarity, SQ = QSQ⊤. Four consequences follow: the eigenvalues s1, s2 are invariant; the deviatoric magnitude r = (s2 − s1)/2 is invariant; the eigenvectors co-rotate, ek,Q = Qek; and the gradient of the invariant scalar s1 co-rotates, ∇Qs1,Q = Q∇s1.

Step 2: the same branch fires in both frames.

Every test that sets the ride flag β is a scalar inequality in the quantities of Step 1: first contact tests ŝ1 < −strim, the optional park tests ŝ1 < scapture, and the terminal condition (29) tests r, ‖∇ŝ1‖, and ŝ1. All are invariant, so β takes the same value at corresponding states.

Step 3: the tangent is equivariant.

The comparison in (27) uses |∇ŝ1⊤ek|, and |(Q∇ŝ1)⊤(Qek)| = |∇ŝ1⊤ek|, so the same eigenvector is selected in both frames. For the sign, suppose tref,Q = Qtprev at some step; then sgn(tref,Q⊤traw,Q) = sgn(tref⊤traw) and tQ = Qt, which supplies the reference for the next step. By induction this holds from the second tracking step onward.

Step 4: the command co-rotates.

The projector transforms as PQ = I − βtQtQ⊤ = QPQ⊤, so PQ∇ŝ1,Q = QP∇ŝ1. The saturation in (28) acts on a vector by scaling its magnitude, which is invariant, and preserving its direction, which co-rotates; the ride term is a scalar multiple of tQ = Qt. Summing the two terms of (28) gives δQ = Qδ, and integrating (30) gives pc,Q(t) = Q(t)pc(t) up to the frame-transport term.

Step 5: the single exception.

The degenerate guard of Section IV-C reads the measured flow directly, and the flow is not objective: in the rotating frame it carries Q̇Q⊤pQ, which has no inertial counterpart. The guard is reachable only on the first tracking step, when r < rband with no previous tangent, and the same flow fallback supplies the sign reference on the first invocation of the ride. At that one instant the resolved sign need not agree between frames. From the second step onward, Step 3 carries the orientation by continuity alone, which is covariant.

Two things I dropped deliberately. The closing sentence of the current proof ("This is the same single-instant caveat the D tracker's own ride mode carries for its own tangent seed") is a comparison, not a step, and it already appears in IV-C and again in VII-C. And the enumeration of which named modes are covered by which argument disappears entirely: with vector saturation, Step 4 covers every branch of (28) in one line, which is a better advertisement for the design than a list.

The remaining page and a bit has to come from the body, and the two candidates that cost nothing scientifically are still the ocean figures, which share a base map and a colorbar and want to be one two-panel figure, and the park-versus-traverse ablation, which is one threshold and two trajectories carried by a single number.
The one thing not to do. If the 75% figure is load-bearing for some other reason, the temptation will be to cut Theorem 2's proof, since it is the longest survivor and its statement reads plausibly on its own. Do not. It is the only formal support for the D tracker's traversal claim, that claim is one of the paper's four stated contributions, and the finite-time bound LΓ/m is meaningless without Step 2. Proposition 2 to supplementary is the safer lever if you truly need another quarter column, and even that costs you the theoretical half of the objectivity result.
