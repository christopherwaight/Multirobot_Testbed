Summary judgement

The cuts sharpen the claim. This is not damage control.

Everything below is decided, so the fix boxes read as instructions rather than options. The two blocking items (Conclusion versus Results on the s1 ocean run, and the unit system) are edits. The structural change is M5: the terminal-capture machinery comes out entirely, which frees about 1.4 columns and, more importantly, replaces a wobble with a thesis.

Before the cut the paper says both trackers can capture and one does it reliably, which reads as an admission. After, it says: the D tracker traverses; the s1 tracker traverses and can certify a terminal core, because s1's minimum is recoverable from a gradient while D's needs a Hessian sign the estimator cannot deliver. That is Remark 1 and Remark 2 paying off together, and it is the strongest sentence the paper could end on.

The second reframe is M4: the ocean sections stop pretending to be quantitative validation and become what they are, a demonstration on measured data. That removes three referee questions the paper cannot answer and saves 0.4 col in the process.

On positioning, the decision is no head-to-head, and the Michini paper supplies the material to make that stick without looking evasive. Details in M6, including the fact that both papers use the same radar record over the same 28-hour window, which the manuscript currently does not mention.

01Strengths

Listed because §12 is aggressive, and these must survive it intact.

Keep

The splitting identity as structural spine

II-D, eq (5)

Deriving both surrogates as the two terms of one exact identity, then assigning a controller to each, is what makes this one paper instead of two. Equation (6) isolating the frame artifact entirely in the spin term is the payoff, and it is set up correctly. I checked (5), (6), (8), (9) and (10) against the double-gyre field by hand; the algebra is right.

Keep

Remark 1, and what it becomes after M5

III-C

A structural impossibility that dictates a design decision (gradient channel, not Newton), then reappears as a measured cost (0.0075 against 0.0016). Theory to design to measurement in three moves.

After the capture cut it does more than that. Remark 1 and Remark 2 together become the explanation for why the two trackers have different terminal behaviour at all: one surrogate's minimum lives in a first derivative of the fit and the other's lives in a second derivative the fit gets structurally wrong. Promote that pairing into the Introduction's contribution list; right now the two remarks sit three subsections apart and never shake hands.

Keep

Minimality, the conic criterion, and the pentagon corollary

III-A

Three results, six lines of proof between them, immediately useful to anyone building a formation. The "any six robots on one circle are singular, move one to the centre" observation is the kind referees remember. Corollary 1's argument is sound: a line meets a circle twice, so no three of five circle points are collinear and the conic through them is unique.

Keep

The closed-loop objectivity experiment

V-D-3, VI-B-2, Fig 6

Same physical flow, two observer frames, 0.025 against 1.219. Most objectivity papers stop at the algebra. The 5c addition justifying Ω against gyre-core vorticity and command authority answers the obvious objection before it is raised.

Keep

Table II(b) against Table II(c) as a diagnostic

VI-B-3

Reporting that far-saddle success is 0.0% at σuv = 0.005 while 90.9% of those same runs settle cleanly at a saddle, then naming the mechanism as a one-time sign inversion rather than a decay toward chance, is the most informative table pair in the paper. It converts a failure into a measurement. The trim does not touch it.

Keep

III-B

Writing out Dxx in true derivatives and stating that four third-order terms "are set to zero by construction, not made small by a good fit" is unusually honest, and after M5 it is load-bearing twice over: it explains the Hessian bias and it explains why the D tracker has no terminal test. Keep the sentence verbatim.

02Major issues

Dispositions settled. The gauge marks severity as submitted; the fix box states the decision.

M1 Blocking

The Conclusion's s1 ocean result is not in the Results

VIII vs VI-D, Abstract

Three places describe the same experiment three different ways:

WhereWhat it says
Abstract"the strain tracker, started on that same ridge, rides the same network structure from the objective strain term alone", i.e. a traverse.
VI-DOne narrated run: rides continuously, terminal test never fires, ends 11.1 km from the nearest of 92 cores. No run is described as converging, holding, or looping.
VIII"the s1 tracker converged to and held a stable local trench loop through the same 28-h record, within 1.4 km of offline ground truth."

"Loop" appears nowhere in Section VI. "Converged to and held" contradicts VI-D's explicit statement that a genuine two-dimensional minimum was never reached. The 1.4 km is the minimum of a twelve-start range, quoted as if it were the result of the run being described.

Decided The Conclusion is wrong; rewrite it to the body. The honest headline is strong on its own: the s1 tracker rode the same ridge network for 28 hours from the objective strain term alone. Drop "loop", "converged to and held", and the 1.4 km. Per M4 the 11.1 km goes too, so VIII carries no ocean distance claim at all.

Do not add a limitations clause about this. VI-D already states it three times in one paragraph: the team rides "without the terminal test firing", "a genuine two-dimensional minimum of s1 was never reached", and the terminal ŝ1 is "the tracked value rather than a certified settle". Introduction contribution 3 scopes it a fourth time, capture on the double gyre and traversal on a field without a stationary point close enough. The fact is disclosed in the two places a reader looks. A fifth statement in VII-E is the redundancy §06 is trying to remove.

The real risk is the opposite one. M4(d) collapses VI-D, and those three phrases are exactly the kind of qualifying language a compression pass deletes. Keep all three. Losing them is the only version of this that a referee could fairly call concealment.
M2 Blocking

The unit system is incoherent

II-E, III-D, V-B, Table I, VI-A, VI-B

Section II-E declares the double gyre non-dimensional. Everything downstream reports that non-dimensional field in SI: σuv and σp grids in m/s and m, "informative to σuv ≈ 0.06 m/s", "tracking error rises from 0.002 m", Table I's Decabot column carrying s−2, m2 and s−1m−1. Meanwhile VI-B-3 gives the same tracking-error quantity as "0.0075" with no unit at all.

Decided Keep the double gyre non-dimensional and strip m and m/s from every double-gyre number, including Table I's Decabot column. Add the non-dimensional note to the Abstract. Keep SI only in the Ocean (phys.) column.

Normalise against two references, not one. Against peak flow (πA ≈ 0.314), σuv = 0.002 is 0.6%, which reads brutal and is the wrong denominator for the failure being described. The tangent seed reads a difference across the formation, and VI-B-3 already gives that span as ≈ 0.12, so the same noise is 1.7% of the signal the decision actually consumes. Report both: peak flow for the field, differential span for the seed. One clause, and it changes how the fragility reads from alarming to legible.
M3 Major

The no-advection assumption is load-bearing and nearly invisible

II-F, missing from VII-E

The modelling premise sits in one clause of II-F: robot motion is "commanded by the control law, not advected by the sampled flow." It never returns. VII-E lists four limitations and this is not among them. On the ocean field the realized speed is roughly 0.5 m/s, the same order as Santa Barbara Channel surface currents, so the 28-hour landfall result is conditional on an authority margin the paper never states.

Decided No partial measurement. Reporting ‖v‖ against command speed without simulating advection invites "so run it with advection", which is the wrong invitation during a page cut.

Instead, reframe it as a scope boundary and say it twice. For the Decabot testbed the assumption is exact, not approximate: the robots are not immersed in anything, the field is encoded and read (per [33]). So it is a scope boundary for the validation platform and an extrapolation caveat for the ocean. Two sentences in VII-E, one line in Future Work.

Do not cite [9] as precedent here. Michini's robots are advected by construction: their eq (1a) carries ui additively, and in the passive straddle state Vi = 0, so L and R drift with the flow. Different precedent or none.
M4 Major

The ocean sections claim validation and can only support demonstration

V-C, VI-C, VI-D

The ocean runs currently carry quantitative furniture they cannot bear: 11.1 km against 92 cores with no null model, a 1.37 to 11.06 km spread with median 3.3 against 2 km grid spacing, 84 of 100 starts. None of it is anchored, because there is no noise budget for the ocean field and no error model for the offline ground truth.

Decided, four parts (a) Delete the 6 km comparison, but replace it, do not just cut it. As written it is the only justification for ρ = 0.105, and it says the radius was enlarged until the tracker agreed with a different dataset. Delete that and 1.4× becomes an unexplained tuned constant, which is worse. Replace with a sampling argument that never mentions 6 km: at ρ = 0.105 the ring is 5.4 km, so the footprint spans roughly five 2 km grid cells, the minimum for a six-point quadratic to see curvature rather than interpolation artifact; at ρ = 0.075 it spans under four. Principled, true, and it drops the provenance.

(b) Keep the truncation-bias analysis, scope it. It makes ρ a tradeoff rather than a free parameter, it is the foundation of Remark 2 and therefore of the whole M5 argument, and it is what Fig 2(b)'s floor curves plot. Your objection holds for the ocean, where M3 is unknown, not for the double gyre, where it is analytic. So confine the claims to the double gyre and add one sentence: no bias bound is claimed on the ocean field, where ρ was set by (a). Twelve words, and a latent hole becomes a stated boundary.

(c) No σuv estimate for the 2 km product. It is not recoverable from the fit: six equations, six unknowns, zero residual by construction. Scope it out and cite [9] when you do. Michini's Section IV closing paragraph scopes out exactly this, in this venue, in the nearest prior work: quantifying uncertainty in real ocean data and its impact on tracking is challenging and outside their scope, which is why they characterise noise on the analytic model instead. That is your paper's structure precisely, and citing it converts an omission into an inherited convention.

(d) Reframe both ocean runs as demonstrations. The claim becomes "the method runs unmodified on measured data for 28 h and produces a path following the dominant FTLE ridge", which is unarguable. The twelve-start spread and the 10×10 jitter grid each collapse to one sentence about the outcome not being knife-edge sensitive to the start. Cut the 11.1 km entirely. Roughly 0.4 col beyond the ledger, and three referee questions disappear with it.
M5 Major

The terminal-capture machinery serves a mode the controller never enters

Thm 1, Thm 2(iii), App A, VII-B, Fig 10

Theorem 1 certifies terminal capture under the signed Newton step. Theorem 2(iii) invokes it at the flow saddle. The paragraph immediately after states that "in practice the tracker continues past the saddle instead", and VI-A confirms it: every run continues past. VII-B then restores the behaviour by a different mechanism entirely, the park step (39), with its own Lyapunov argument, disabled by default. A theorem, a 470-word proof, part of a second theorem, two proof steps, a discussion subsection and a full figure, all for a terminal behaviour that appears in one reported run produced by a fourth control case that is off.

Decided: cut the whole case. Full inventory in §03. About 1.4 columns, the largest single saving in the plan.

Three things survive the cut and must not be swept up in it. (1) The degenerate branch of (27) stays as a well-definedness guard, since the trench frame is undefined when the eigenvalues share a sign and the law needs something; one sentence, no theorem, no certificate. (2) The s1 terminal test (30) and its Lyapunov certificate (37) stay. That is not the park step, it fires, and it is demonstrated on all six double-gyre runs. It becomes the only terminal behaviour in the paper, which makes it more load-bearing, not less, and is why −4strim now needs its twelve words of justification (§05). (3) The saddle of the D field stays as the object the traverse passes through.

What the cut buys. The paper stops arguing with itself about whether its controller stops. TRAP cores, eddy cores and other non-critical-point targets go to the follow-on paper, with one Future Work sentence pointing there.
M6 Major

Positioning against [9]: no head-to-head, but the gap has to be filled

VII-A, VI-C, ref [14]

The head-to-head is off, and the Michini paper is the reason. Three independent grounds, all citable from their own text:

GroundFrom [9]
Their initialization is the methodAssumption 1 requires d(xC(0), BS) < ε with L and R already on opposite sides. Running them from your off-structure starts puts them outside their own theorem, which is a strawman. Running you from a straddling start discards your acquisition claim.
Different plantsTheir robots are advected (eq 1a); yours are not (II-F). One simulator cannot host both without altering one of them.
The noise arithmetic would misleadTheir Table I gives 74% at I = 0.005, which is σ = 0.1 against a mean flow of 0.42, roughly 24% relative noise. Yours degrades near 3%. But their criterion is "straddle maintained after 1.5 s", yours is "reach a designated saddle over up to 500 steps", and they average over measurement history where you take one synchronous sample. A reader takes away "the 2014 method is ten times more noise-robust", which is false and unfixable in a caption.

But VII-A currently makes performance claims against [9] to [11] with no evidence, and a simulation-only paper still has to answer "compared to what?". Three cheap moves close it without running anything.

Decided (a) Say explicitly that it is the same field. Michini's Section V-D uses Santa Barbara Channel HF radar on a 2 km grid over a 28-h window, May 16 2012 08:00 GMT to May 17 12:00 GMT. Yours is May 16 to 17 2012, 29 hourly frames covering 28 h, 2 km, same network. Same record, same window. The manuscript does not mention this. A referee who knows [9] spots it immediately, and silence reads worse than acknowledgment. Said out loud it becomes an asset: the fields are directly comparable by eye, at zero experimental cost.

(b) Make the comparison structural, not empirical. A five or six row table on stated assumptions: initialization requirement, structure identity known in advance, sensing model (single synchronous sample versus measurement history), advection, what is estimated (velocity, gradient, curvature), terminal behaviour. Every cell sourced from published assumptions, so nothing is arguable. Roughly 0.3 col, and it replaces VII-A's prose rather than adding to it.

(c) Cite their own reported limitation. Their Discussion states that the robots "tend to veer away from the tracked LCS as they approach a local saddle/hyperbolic point", observed in both the flow tank and the ocean runs, and attributed to the flow reversing across the saddle. That is precisely the behaviour your method is built around: your D tracker traverses through the crossing and your s1 tracker settles at it. Quoting a paper's own stated limitation is the least adversarial comparison that exists.

(d) [14] to arXiv. Separate from the baseline question and not negotiable. It carries eq (38), the 0.3 s settling separation that licenses the kinematic abstraction in (31), and the prior-art framing. A referee who cannot see it cannot check the stability analysis's premise.
M7 Major

Abstract, I, III-A

Six is minimal for the unconstrained quadratic model, correctly qualified in 5c. But both test fields are incompressible, so the headline count is minimal for a model class the paper never needs, and the defence offered is two assertions: that measured surface currents are "weakly divergent", cited to nothing, and that "the fitted divergence is a live diagnostic rather than an assumption", never reported anywhere in Section VI.

Decided Delete "weakly divergent" and delete the divergence-diagnostic promise. Replace with the presupposition argument, which is thematically consistent and stronger: the Introduction already sells "neither pre-straddling an assumed manifold nor accumulation of data points", and this extends the same line to "and no constitutive assumption about the field". The sixth robot becomes a deliberate refusal to presuppose rather than a defensive footnote. Tie it back to the Introduction explicitly.

No appendix. The five-robot counting is one line and is already nearly in your text: imposing ∇·v = 0 and its two first derivatives removes three of twelve coefficients, so five robots supply ten equations for nine unknowns. Three sentences in III-A. Adding an appendix during a page cut is the wrong direction, and by the paper's own sidequest test it fails.

10Length: where this sits against T-RO practice

There is no published corpus statistic for T-RO section lengths, so the norm column is a referee's working expectation for a methods-plus-validation paper in this journal, not a measured mean. The hard facts bracket it: T-RO regular papers may not exceed 20 formatted pages, and mandatory overlength charges apply to every page beyond 12.

Two numbers worth holding. At 18.5 pages you pay overlength on 6.5 pages; at 16.2 on 4.2. The modal T-RO regular paper sits at 12 to 16 pages precisely because the charge starts at 12, so an 18.5-page submission reads as long before a referee has read a word.
